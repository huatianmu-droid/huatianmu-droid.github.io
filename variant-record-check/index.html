Why identity comes before a measurement

A photograph may contain a useful ruler reading while showing a different size from the one in your shortlist. Copying that number into the selected item’s row creates false certainty. A clear photograph supports only what it actually shows; it does not establish a connection to a different variant.

Keep a stable item reference and record the exact selected size and colour identifier. If a source uses a colour name without a code, preserve the source wording. Do not silently treat similar words as the same variant. The tool trims surrounding whitespace but otherwise compares the text exactly, including letter case.

Interpret the result narrowly

“Same recorded text” means the two entered strings match. It does not prove that the underlying products are identical. “Conflicting text” means the strings differ, not that one is wrong. “Missing evidence” means at least one side has no entry. Two blanks are still missing information, never a match.

In the demonstration, Demo-A and Navy-01 agree, but M and L conflict. The appropriate next action is to confirm which size each measurement describes. Do not average the measurements or choose the more convenient number. If a field was merely a typing error, keep a dated correction that explains the earlier and replacement text.

Attach evidence without overstating it

For a new measurement, record the source, the date, the method, and how the pictured label connects to the selected variant. If that connection is absent, leave it unresolved. A seller reply may clarify a label while still leaving the measurement method unknown; preserve both facts separately.
